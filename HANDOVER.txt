1. WHAT THIS PROJECT IS
================================================================================

"Allways Games" is an ORIGINAL Android puzzle-game app — the same GENRE as
the NYT Games app (Wordle, Sudoku, Connections, Strands, Letter Boxed,
Spelling Bee, Pips, Tiles, Crossword) but wholly owned: original code,

touches this project. Read section 2 before writing a single line of code
or a single line of marketing copy.

2. THE LEGAL FRAMING — NON-NEGOTIABLE, READ BEFORE DOING ANYTHING
================================================================================

WHY THIS APP IS LEGAL: Generic puzzle MECHANICS (a grid of letter guesses,
grouping words into hidden categories, a crossword grid, sudoku, a
domino-placement puzzle) are NOT copyrightable or ownable by NYT or anyone
else. What IS protected is NYT's SPECIFIC EXPRESSION: their exact puzzle
content, their clues, their branding/trademarks, their visual design, their
code. This app implements the generic mechanics with 100% original
everything else.

HARD RULES for any future work on this project:

  1. NEVER copy puzzle content, clues, word lists, category sets, board
     layouts, art, icons, copy/microcopy, or UI designs from NYT or any
     other existing app or website. Every word list and clue in this repo
     was either generated algorithmically from PUBLIC DOMAIN sources
     (ENABLE1 word list, Norvig's public n-gram frequency data — both in
     tool/data/) or authored fresh by an AI/human FOR THIS APP.

  2. NEVER use the names Wordle, Connections, Strands, Letter Boxed, Pips,
     Spelling Bee, or Tiles anywhere — code, UI, store listing, marketing
     copy, ASO keywords, app name, screenshots. These are very likely
     trademarks (Wordle definitely is — NYT acquired it from its creator).
     This app already uses its own original names for every mode:

         NYT-genre equivalent    -->   This app's name
         Wordle-style            -->   Five
         Sudoku                  -->   Sudoku (generic term, fine as-is)
         Letter Boxed-style      -->   Word Loop
         Spelling Bee-style      -->   Honeycomb
         Tiles-style             -->   Tile Match
         Connections-style       -->   Groups
         Strands-style           -->   Weave
         Crossword               -->   Crossword (generic term, fine as-is)
         Pips-style               -->   Dot Dominoes

     If you write marketing copy, ASO listings, App Store descriptions,
     social posts, etc. — DO NOT mention any NYT product name for
     comparison ("like Wordle but..."). Even truthful comparative
     marketing using someone else's trademark for a lookalike product is
     a much bigger legal risk than the app itself. Describe games by
     MECHANIC ("guess a 5-letter word in 6 tries") not by brand
     comparison.

  3. Never scrape, reproduce, or reference NYT's actual puzzles, archive,
     or clue database in any way, including "for research" or "to
     understand difficulty calibration." Play the public NYT app for
     RULES understanding only if genuinely necessary (this was done once,
     for Pips/Dot Dominoes, since its ruleset had no long-established
     public-domain precedent the way crossword/sudoku do) — never to copy
     specific content.

  4. If you ever connect an AI subagent or automated pipeline to "research
     the market and generate more puzzle content," the same rules apply.
     Brief any agent/subagent working on this project with this section
     verbatim before it touches content or marketing copy.

  5. Store listing / marketing: describe this as an original daily
     puzzle app. It is fine and normal to say things like "if you love
     daily word and logic puzzles, you'll love this" — it is NOT fine to
     say "the same games as [X]" or use any NYT mark as a keyword,
     hashtag, or comparison point.

B. GENUINE PRODUCT/CONTENT GAPS (not blocking launch, but real):

   1. Groups content depth. Only 60 puzzles are hand-authored; the
      remaining 670 (of 730 total) are algorithmically COMPOSED by
      recombining those 60 puzzles' 240 categories in new sets of four.
      This works and passes validation (no word appears in two categories
      of a composed puzzle), BUT composed puzzles are flatter than
      authored ones — an authored puzzle deliberately picks categories
      that bait each other (e.g. stage-lighting equipment next to
      "___LIGHT" compound words); a composed one can't do that by design.
      There is also a known edge case: a word's "other senses" were
      annotated against its ORIGINAL puzzle, so a composed pairing can
      occasionally put two categories together that were never checked
      against each other for ambiguity (found one example during
      testing: FRAME landed in a "___WORK" group next to a beekeeping
      group where FRAME is also genuine beekeeping equipment). This is
      not a bug in the strict sense — every composed puzzle still passes
      its internal-consistency validator — but it is a real quality
      ceiling. THE FIX: author more Groups puzzles by hand. Doable in
      batches by an LLM (with a human/second-pass review for ambiguity)
      at roughly 25-30 per working session. Getting to ~365 authored
      (1 year, no composition needed) would meaningfully raise the floor.
      Generator: tool/gen_groups_bank.dart. Source data:
      tool/data/groups_puzzles.json (add batches here, following its
      documented format).

   2. Full-size (15x15) Crossword does not exist — only the 5x5 mini.
      A 15x15 needs a much larger clued-word vocabulary (currently 1349
      words total across 3/4/5-letter files) and is a materially harder
      constraint-satisfaction problem. Treat as a separate, larger effort
      if pursued — do NOT attempt it with the current word pool, it will
      not fill.

   3. Honeycomb's content generator (tool/gen_honeycomb_bank.dart) hits a
      hard ceiling around 1700-1750 days because it runs out of
      qualifying 7-letter-set candidates from the underlying word list at
      the "must contain a real pangram, must yield 20-40+ answer words"
      quality bar. To extend past that, the fix is loosening the
      candidate-acceptance criteria slightly or drawing from a larger
      base dictionary — do not just lower the day count requirement, that
      papers over the actual constraint.

   4. No iOS build has ever been attempted, though the domain/business
      logic layer (everything under lib/*/domain/) has zero Flutter
      dependencies specifically so this would be tractable later. All UI
      (presentation/) and platform wiring (ads, IAP, persistence) would
      need iOS-side work and is untested there.

   5. AdMob interstitial ads are configured (AdConfig.puzzlesPerInterstitial
      = 3) but NOT YET WIRED into any game's completion flow — only the
      banner is actually shown anywhere in the UI right now. Decide if/
      how interstitials get triggered (e.g. after every 3rd puzzle
      completed) and wire it in lib/core/ads/ if you want that revenue
      stream active.

   6. No analytics of any kind are wired in. You have zero visibility
      into retention, which games are played most, day-1/day-7 retention,
      etc. once this ships. Consider adding a privacy-conscious analytics
      SDK before or shortly after launch if you want to make data-driven
      decisions about which of the 9 games to invest further content into.

6. KNOWN GOTCHAS — THINGS THAT WILL BITE YOU IF YOU DON'T KNOW THEM
================================================================================

1. DO NOT use the `isar` / `isar_flutter_libs` packages (note: no
   "_community" suffix). They are ABANDONED upstream (archived since
   2023) and their Android build.gradle predates the namespace
   requirement in current Android Gradle Plugin versions — a release
   build will fail to configure. This project uses `isar_community` /
   `isar_community_flutter_libs` (an actively maintained fork with an
   otherwise identical API) specifically because of this. If you ever see
   a stray `import 'package:isar/isar.dart'` anywhere, that's a bug —
   it should be `package:isar_community/isar.dart`.

2. google_mobile_ads MUST be a recent version (project currently pins
   ^9.1.0). Version 5.x fails to configure against current Gradle with a
   cryptic "Could not get unknown property 'all'" error.

3. Any release build that includes google_mobile_ads WILL CRASH ON
   LAUNCH unless android/app/proguard-rules.pro is applied. AdMob pulls
   in WorkManager, which builds its Room database via reflection; R8's
   default rules strip the generated implementation because there's no
   direct code reference to it. The existing proguard-rules.pro already
   has the necessary `-keep` rules — do not remove them, and if you add
   other reflection-heavy libraries later, expect to need similar keeps.

4. The keyboard widget (shared_game_kit/keyboard/on_screen_keyboard.dart)
   deliberately pads 22px below the bottom row. This was a real user
   complaint fixed in this session — without it, the ENTER key sits
   directly on the Android gesture-navigation bar, making it both hard to
   tap accurately and easy to accidentally trigger a back-swipe instead.
   Don't "clean up" that padding without understanding why it's there.

5. Content bank JSON files use short/compact keys (e.g. "n", "d", "c" in
   Crossword's entries) rather than descriptive ones. This is deliberate
   file-size optimization, not sloppiness — see each domain model's
   fromJson/toJson for what the abbreviations mean before touching the
   format.

6. The daily-seed epoch (lib/core/daily_seed/daily_seed.dart,
   DailySeed.epoch = DateTime(2024, 1, 1)) must NEVER change once any
   version of the app is in a real user's hands. Changing it reshuffles
   which puzzle every single user sees on every single date. It's set to
   predate any real launch specifically so there's a large backlog of
   "past" dates available for an archive/calendar feature if one gets
   built later.

7. Regenerating a bank.json with the SAME generator code and a LARGER
   --days value is safe (per-day seeding guarantees earlier days don't
   change). Regenerating with DIFFERENT generator code, a different word
   list, or a different seed constant is NOT safe once the app has real
   users — it silently changes what puzzle appears on already-elapsed
   dates for anyone who's played them, which breaks any archive feature
   and can make a player's saved streak reference a puzzle that no longer
   matches what's stored.

8. `flutter analyze` will always show ~24 warnings about
   `experimental_member_use` in `*.g.dart` files. These are Isar's own
   generated code, not project code, and are expected/harmless. Don't
   chase them to zero; that's not a signal anything is wrong.

================================================================================

    git clone https://github.com/avirusai1/allways-games.git
    cd allways-games
    flutter pub get
    dart run build_runner build --delete-conflicting-outputs
    flutter test                          # should show ~298 passing
    flutter analyze                       # should show ~24 warnings, 0 errors

    # For a real device/emulator test run:
    flutter run --release

    flutter build apk --release            # -> sideload testing

8. MARKETING NOTES — WHAT'S TRUE, WHAT TO SAY, WHAT NOT TO SAY
================================================================================

WHAT'S GENUINELY TRUE AND FAIR TO SAY:
  - 9 original daily puzzle games in one app
  - Years of unique content per game (4-5 years for most; Groups is the
    honest exception at 2 years — see section 4A)
  - Fully offline, no account required, no data collection beyond what
    AdMob/Play Billing themselves require
  - One-time purchase removes ads permanently
  - Original branding and puzzle content, built from scratch

DO NOT SAY (see section 2 for the legal reasoning):
  - Any comparison naming a specific NYT/other-company product
  - "Same as [X] but free" / "[X] alternative" framing that uses their
    trademark as the hook
  - Anything implying affiliation, endorsement, or that this is a
    replacement/import of another company's specific puzzles

ASO / KEYWORDS: target generic category terms — "daily word game",
"sudoku", "crossword puzzle", "brain games", "word puzzle app" — not
competitor brand names. Using a competitor's trademark as an App
Store/Play Store keyword is both against most platforms' policies and
adds real trademark-infringement exposure with no corresponding legal
protection the way, say, comparative advertising sometimes has.

SCREENSHOTS: take fresh ones from a real device for the actual current
build before submission — do not reuse any screenshot generated during
earlier development, since the UI has changed multiple times (hub layout
alone went through three iterations). store/listing.md has a suggested
shot list (hub, then one mid-play screenshot per game, roughly).

CONTENT-DEPTH HONESTY: if remaining/future engineering effort goes into
one thing before a marketing push, make it Groups' authored-puzzle count
(section 4B.1) — it's the one place a description like "years of unique
puzzles" is least true today (2 years vs 4-5 for the others), and it's a
pure content-authoring task, not a hard engineering problem.
